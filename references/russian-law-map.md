# Russian / EAEU Legal Domain Map

This is a **research map, not a statement that every item applies**. The agent must check the current law and the actual site's business model on every run. It must not use this list as a substitute for current legal research.

## 1. Official source starting points

Use these as entry points, then locate a direct document/provision:

- Official publication of Russian legal acts: <https://publication.pravo.gov.ru/>
- Legal information / official legal portal: <https://pravo.gov.ru/>
- Roskomnadzor: <https://rkn.gov.ru/>
- Rospotrebnadzor: <https://rospotrebnadzor.ru/> and the consumer-rights portal <https://zpp.rospotrebnadzor.ru/>
- Federal Antimonopoly Service (advertising): <https://fas.gov.ru/>
- Federal Tax Service (tax and cash register): <https://nalog.gov.ru/>
- Eurasian Economic Commission (EAEU acts / technical regulations): <https://eec.eaeunion.org/>
- Official publication of Supreme Court decisions and court information: use the relevant official court domain and direct act/case page.
- ConsultantPlus / GARANT: useful for navigation and consolidated texts; high-impact claims should be cross-checked against official legal publications and amendment records.

If a link has moved or is inaccessible, search by the exact act title/number/date and record the fallback. A homepage link alone is not evidence of the provision.

## 2. Personal data and privacy

Research at least the current version of:

- Federal Law No. 152-FZ of 27.07.2006 “On Personal Data”.
- Federal Law No. 149-FZ of 27.07.2006 “On Information, Information Technologies and Information Protection”.
- Relevant current provisions of the Code of Administrative Offences (KoAP RF).
- Current Government resolutions, Roskomnadzor procedures and regulator guidance relevant to the actual processing.

Verify, rather than assume:

- whether the business is a personal-data operator and for which purposes;
- whether notice/registration with Roskomnadzor is required and whether a claimed exception is truly available;
- privacy-policy accessibility and completeness;
- purpose-specific legal basis and consent requirements in the current version of the law;
- separate consent where the current law requires it;
- localisation rules for collection/recording/systematisation/accumulation/storage and exceptions;
- cross-border transfers and required notifications/conditions;
- processor/controller roles and contracts;
- retention, deletion, subject requests and breach notification;
- security measures and access controls;
- cookies, analytics IDs, IP addresses, call tracking and indirect identifiers based on actual use, not blanket assumptions.

Do not assume all cookies automatically constitute personal data; do not assume cookies are exempt. Explain the factual link to an identifiable person and the actual processing purpose.

## 3. Authentication and access to information

Research current Article 8 of Law No. 149-FZ and applicable current KoAP provisions if the site uses authentication, accounts, gated content or user-specific access. Check scope and statutory exceptions before making a conclusion.

Do not state that Google OAuth, Apple ID or all foreign authentication are prohibited on every website. First establish:

- whether authentication is present;
- whether access to the information is conditional on authentication;
- whether the resource and owner fall within the exact rule;
- which methods are allowed in the current statutory text;
- current effective date and exact sanction, if relevant.

A publicly accessible catalog with no user accounts may have a different result from a service where information is available only after login. Record the reasoning and residual uncertainty.

## 4. Consumer information and distance selling

Start with:

- Law of the Russian Federation No. 2300-1 of 07.02.1992 “On Protection of Consumer Rights”.
- Civil Code of the Russian Federation, including provisions relevant to sale, retail sale and contract formation.
- Current Government rules and other regulations governing retail/distance sales as of the audit date.
- Current product-specific acts where applicable.

Check:

- legal identity and contact information of the actual seller;
- B2B versus B2C intent and whether the sale is for personal/family/domestic use;
- accurate product identity, model, specifications, price, discounts, availability and limitations;
- whether buttons/messages create or imply an order, reservation or contract;
- order confirmation and pre-contract disclosures;
- delivery, pickup, payment, returns, exchange, warranty and complaint routes if offered;
- whether actual phone-originated sales remain subject to particular retail/cash-register rules;
- duties relating to conformity documents, labels or registry links for categories where applicable.

Never reuse historical retail-sale rules after replacement without checking the current status. Verify amendments and transition provisions, especially where a new rule has recently become effective.

## 5. Advertising and marketing

Start with current Federal Law No. 38-FZ “On Advertising”, the KoAP RF and current rules for online-ad labeling/data reporting where relevant.

Determine case-by-case whether an item is ordinary information about the site's own goods, advertising, or a third-party paid placement. Verify requirements that actually apply to the placement and actor. Check:

- substantiation of product claims and comparisons;
- discounts and reference/old prices;
- reviews and endorsements;
- promotional calls/SMS/email/messenger messages and consent requirements;
- online advertising identifiers/reporting where relevant;
- prohibited placements/platforms, as currently applicable;
- third-party pixels and data transfers.

Do not categorise every catalog block as advertising automatically. Preserve the factual context and the exact placement.

## 6. Payments, cash register and tax

Start with current Federal Law No. 54-FZ on the use of cash-register equipment, applicable tax legislation and official FNS explanations. Establish who receives money, when payment happens, payment method, seller type, online/offline status and who issues the receipt.

Do not infer that KKT is unnecessary merely because the lead originated by phone or the site has no checkout. Escalate tax-regime, VAT, invoicing and business-accounting questions to the owner's accountant when facts are missing.

## 7. Goods, product conformity and EAEU technical rules

Create a SKU/product-category matrix before determining legal obligations. Consider, only where product classification supports it:

- Federal Law No. 184-FZ “On Technical Regulation”.
- EAEU technical regulations including TR CU 004/2011 (low-voltage equipment), TR CU 020/2011 (electromagnetic compatibility), TR CU 010/2011 (machinery/equipment safety), TR CU 009/2011 (perfumery and cosmetic products), and other applicable acts.
- Applicable marking, manuals, warnings, manufacturer/importer details, Russian-language information and conformity records.

These are starting points, not a blanket claim that all rules apply to all tanning equipment, lamps, cosmetics or accessories. Determine classification, exemptions, voltage/technical characteristics, intended use, producer/importer/distributor role and conformity route. Verify whether product information must link to current records in official registers under the law in force at launch. Never invent certificates, declarations or registry entries.

## 8. Language of public information

Check current requirements for Russian-language information, including applicable amendments to language and consumer-information legislation and exceptions for trademarks, brand names or other specified cases. Distinguish required consumer/product information from proper names and branding. Do not blindly translate trademarks or claim that every Latin-character string is prohibited.

## 9. Intellectual property

Check Part IV of the Civil Code and applicable special rules for:

- logos, trademarks and brand use;
- product photos and videos;
- descriptions, manuals and technical documents;
- fonts, icons, illustrations, templates and code assets;
- customer reviews and user-submitted materials.

A publicly available image is not automatically licensed for commercial reuse. Record the asset's source/licence where verifiable.

## 10. Security and website operations

Map legal requirements separately from recommended security practices. Depending on actual processing and system design, consider:

- access control, administrator privileges and audit logs;
- secret handling and credential storage;
- secure cookies, session management, CSRF/XSS/SQL injection protections where applicable;
- data backups, retention and secure deletion;
- hosting and backup locations;
- supplier access and processor agreements;
- incident response and notification timelines.

Do not label every OWASP issue a legal violation without identifying the applicable legal duty. Conversely, a legally required security outcome may need engineering controls beyond a checklist.

## 11. Future feature gates

Before activating any of these, repeat a focused legal review:

- customer accounts/authentication or gated content;
- online checkout, acquiring, instalments or subscriptions;
- CRM and sales-history storage;
- analytics, behavioural profiling or product recommendations;
- cookies, call tracking, recording, email/SMS/messenger outreach;
- delivery service, courier integration, marketplace/aggregator;
- file uploads, reviews or user-generated content;
- cross-border provider or new hosting/database region.

For each feature identify data, purpose, role, vendor, storage location, legal basis, disclosures, contracts, retention and user rights.

## 12. Legal monitoring seeds — not pre-decided conclusions

At each audit, search current amendments and effective dates for the acts identified above. If recent changes are reported in 2025–2026 or later, verify them from official publication records before relying on a summary. Specifically re-check recent changes affecting personal-data consent/localisation, remote sales and conformity information, Russian-language consumer information, internet advertising, authentication and administrative sanctions. This list does not assert that a particular change applies to the site; it is a reminder to verify current law.
